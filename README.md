# Hotel Revenue Performance Analysis
## 🎯Objective: 
This project analyzes historical daily revenue data to evaluate hotel financial performance and operational efficiency. Designed to support revenue management, it focuses on key operational metrics, room revenue composition and leakage, and incorporates forecasting to optimize pricing strategies and maximize profitabilitym in Q2, 2024 that feaaturing a diverse lineup of MICE events, sports, and entertainment activities over the April–June. 
## 📄 Case Study: 
- **Property**: The Prime Köln - 4 stars Hotel
- **Inventory**: 500 Rooms
- **Location**: Köln Central
- **Period**: Q1 2024
## 🛠️ Tool & Data: 
- Power BI Desktop – for data modeling and visualization.
- Dataset – captures daily hotel revenue performance in Q1 2024 (including RevPAR, ADR, costs, occupancy, etc.),
## 📊 Data Analysis & Strategic Insights
### 🏢 Hotel Operational Performance
<img width="1298" height="736" alt="Screenshot 2026-05-04 140709" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0cff6185-df8f-4b50-9b79-0771d1cf0793" />

Overall, the hotel achieved a strong 80.2% profit margin in Q1 2024, generating €7.7M in revenue and €6.2M in GOP from 44,318 rooms, with high guest satisfaction (4.6 rating)

- Weekly revenue stayed stable at €0.5M–€0.6M, with sharp dips at month-start and end (down to €0.18M and €0.08M in March), while margins remained stable at 79%–81.5%.
- Saturday leads daily revenue (€1.12M), Thursday was the lowest (€1.09M), with other days remaining consistent between €1.09M and €1.10M.
- Seasonally, January was focused on the start and middle of the week (Tue peak), February shifts to Thursdays, and March becomes weekend-driven (Sat peak, Fri/Sun were shoulder days).
- Costs remained flat (€0.1M–€0.5M); revenue peaks at 78% occupancy but drops at 81%, suggesting discounting or lower-yield mix. The break-even point (BEP) was around 76%, with higher occupancy driving extra costs.
- Higher review scores drive revenue. The revenue increased from ~€81K at 4.4 to more than €90K+ at 4.7. The optimal “sweet spot” was around 4.6, with revenue between €82K and €88K.

### 🧮 Room Revenue Breakdown and Leakage
<img width="1276" height="728" alt="Screenshot 2026-05-04 140653" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/93e5b1cf-01e0-4a3d-a4c4-f734ccb744fd" />

In general, rooms generated €4M in revenue, with average occupancy at 78%, ADR around €125, RevPAR at €96, TRevPAR at €175, and an out-of-order room rate of 2.6%.

- ADR remained stable within €120–€129, peaking in early March (€129) and early February (€128). RevPAR followed a similar trend at €90–€103, with peaks in the first week of February (€101) and March (€103).
- Non-room revenue (F&B, spa, laundry, etc.) consistently accounts for about 44–46% of total room-related revenue each month. Average RevPAR ranges from €94–€98, while TRevPAR is significantly higher at €174.5–€175.6.
- The hotel consistently achieves a 76% revenue performance rate, though it dips to 74% in Week 5 and a quarterly low of 73% in Week 6. With a fixed inventory of 500 rooms, this indicates that 24%–27% of potential revenue capacity remains unutilized.
- Unsold rooms are the main source (87.61%) of revenue loss. OOO accounts for 10.84%, while no-shows (1.03%) and cancellations (0.52%) are minimal.

### 📌 Highlight Insight
**Key Strengths**: 
- Strong profitability (80.2% GOP margin)
- Solid ancillary revenue (45% from other departments)
- Diverse Market Mix: Balanced corporate (weekdays) and leisure (weekend) demand 
- Positive link between satisfaction and pricing (4.6 point)

**Areas for Improvement**
  - Unsold rooms account high rate of revenue loss
  - Out-of-Order (OOO) rooms caused of revenue leak
  - A drop in Achievement Rate in late March reveals a delayed reaction to softening market demand
  - Performance begins to decline beyond 80% occupancy, indicating risks of over-discounting or high marketing cost to fill remaining capacity.
### 💡 Strategic Recommendations
- Reduce OOO revenue leakage by **optimizing inventory availability**
- Apply a **tiered pricing strategy**, gradually increasing rates toward the €138 upper bound at 60% and 80% occupancy to **maximize willingness to pay (WTP)**
- Use the low cancellation rate (0.52%) to implement a ~2% **overbooking strategy** on peak nights to ensure full capacity, avoid revenue loss from last-minute cancellation and no-show booking
- Launch **“Early Bird”** & **promotions** offers for low-demand weeks in April and May, **"last-minute"** pricing during peak demand periods Especially in June
- Implement a 3-night **Minimum Length of Stay (MLOS) booking** and require a **booking deposit** during major events (especially uro 2024 and The Tire Cologne), complemented by value-added packages (e.g., breakfast) to enhance TRevPAR.
- Optimize room yield by incentivizing staff to **upsell and convert standard bookings into premium categories at check-in**
- Enforce a **stricter cancellation policy** (No refund, etc.) to reduce last-minute cancellations
- **Close out low-yield channels** (e.g., OTAs) during event periods and prioritize high-margin Direct Bookings and Best Available Rate (BAR) rates to avoid booking commission fee for third parties and optimize revenue
- Actively **monitor the compset's pricing strategy** to identify **price floors and ceilings**, and adjust rates accordingly to maintain competitiveness and optimize revenue, particularly during the shoulder and peak periods in June.
